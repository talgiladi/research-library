### [Runway’s co-CEO sees video models outgrowing the screen](https://www.thedeepview.com/articles/runway-ceo-sees-a-much-bigger-future-for-video-ai)

Cristóbal Valenzuela sat down with Deep View after Wednesday’s Runway AI Summit and argued that video models are generalization machines, doing next-frame prediction under the same scaling laws as next-token language models, which is why a system trained to make film can also drive a robot. The two launches he wants held together are Runway Ads, an agent that writes on-brand video and stills, publishes the variants a person approves to Meta, Google, and TikTok, and then regenerates around whatever actually earned the spend (on Runway’s own accounts, weekly volume went from 77 ads to about 900 while return on ad spend doubled), and Praxis-1, an open-weight world action model that starts from that same video pretraining and adapts to a new robot body with light fine-tuning, with the weights still months from a public release. He puts fully world-model policies in live production 12 to 18 months out, and the constraint he names is speed on the robot, not a shortage of video to train on. Hollywood, he says, spent a year and a half insisting a model would never match taste and has now reached acceptance, while individual creators are still scattered across the earlier stages. The line that will travel is geographic: China, in his telling, is “world-model-pilled” and the US is still “LLM-pilled,” with roughly 70% of Chinese token use going to video, and he treats Solaris, the August model that renders an interface frame by frame, as an early step toward generated operating systems rather than another chatbot. Sponsored: the same edition pitches Delta, from the team behind Zed, as review that keeps the agent’s plan beside the diff.

## Agent memory

### [Trained Agentic Context Management](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02404)

Long-context work usually either trains a model with a huge window or designs an elaborate harness around a short one. This paper does neither: it finetunes Qwen3.6-35B-A3B on the thinnest possible agent loop, a tool that re-invokes the model with any prompt plus a tool that reads a token range from the input, over a diverse synthetic dataset. With only 8,000 tokens of context the small model matches GPT-5.4 given a million tokens on OOLONG-synth once documents pass 40K tokens. The interesting claim is that agentic context management—learning when and what to re-read—can substitute for native long context, at least on that synthetic long-document benchmark.

## Agent harness

### [Fast Models, Slow Evidence: A Paired and Self-Audited Evaluation of System-1 Decision Models for LLM Agent Harnesses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02267)

An agent harness spends most of its life on tiny typed decisions: which model to call, which tool to pick, whether retrieved text is relevant, whether an input looks like an injection. System-1 decision models promise to answer those in one forward pass with class probabilities instead of another LLM call. This evaluation pairs an open-weight model (Laya) with a hosted one (Jev) on 11 decision points built from 18 public sources—7,283 base cases plus 6,640 robustness variants—with byte-identical inputs and cross-hardware, cross-day checks. Jev wins on 9 of 11 points by 10.8 to 46.0 points; neither beats chance on zero-shot model routing, and they tie on RAG relevance gating. Laya flips 30% of answers when option order is reversed and collapses when candidates proliferate (31% at 50 nearest-neighbour tools, versus 98% for Jev when the correct tool is unique). More importantly, the authors audit their own pipeline and find four analysis mistakes that had inflated deployment claims, including a reported 23.9% cost saving that was really 4.3% once a pre-screen cost was counted, and an injection “channel effect” that disappeared under channel-native content.

### [Choosing Before Acting: Comparative Value Estimation for Long-Horizon Tool-Use Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02330)

Long tool traces make final-outcome rewards a poor teacher: credit is diluted across many steps, and honest step labels usually need a human, another LLM, or expensive extra rollouts. CITA’s pitch is that a tool-use agent should estimate the long-horizon value of the next invocation before it runs, which needs comparative supervision over alternatives under the same context—something logged trajectories never contain, because they only record the call that was taken. Comparative Inference for Tool-use Agents trains a Comparative Inference Model from paired signals that mix observed tool behavior, a Bayesian tool-graph simulator, and LLM semantic comparisons, so the model learns how likely a candidate next call is to help final success. Across three tool-use benchmarks and several backbone LLMs it lifts Tool F1 and task success, and follow-up analysis says the step-level value estimates for comparative choices are actually accurate.

### [When Terminal-Agent Training Stalls: Demystifying Data Generation and Verification Challenge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02405)

Using a frontier model such as Claude Opus as a meta-agent to invent terminal tasks and verifiers for RL is now common, but a runnable Docker image and a green test suite do not mean the training pipeline is faithful. This paper’s meta-agent pipeline surfaces three failure classes—benchmark invalidity, harness brittleness, and reward misalignment—and shows how easy it is to look successful while the band of solvable tasks is secretly model-specific. Prompt redesign and longer context raise baseline solvability 5.6×, yet a 9B model still saturates at 81.3% mean pass@2 within 20 steps on Opus-generated tasks; swapping in hard tasks drops that to 20.6% with the training setup unchanged. The practical warning is that meta-agent reliability needs solvability-band calibration, verifier audits, and infrastructure error accounting as first-class metrics, not post-mortems after training stalls.

## LLM security

### [Intent-Hiding Jailbreaks: An Information-Theoretic Framework for Compositional Attacks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02302)

A harmful request that is refused on its own can succeed when it is buried inside a larger, apparently benign query. This paper treats those compositional intent-hiding jailbreaks information-theoretically: each task gets an estimated probability of being judged harmful, the average over the whole collection is the prior, and the average over a selected bundle that still contains the target is the posterior. Choosing auxiliaries so prior and posterior match—prior-posterior matching—leaves the estimated intent unchanged even though the harmful target stays in the bundle. Exact matching under a bundle-size constraint is hard; they give a water-filling optimum for fractional weights and characterize the smallest bundle that meets a safety threshold, then evaluate both query-independent and query-dependent settings across bundle sizes, query generators, and several open-source models. Compositional queries do beat the direct-request baseline under the budgets tried, but as bundles grow, response-level preservation of the target behavior tends to fall.

### [MIRROR: Multipath Quorum Integrity for LLM Multi-Agent Communication](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02349)

Once agents talk to each other, an attacker who never compromises a model can still rewrite messages in flight—an Agent-in-the-Middle attack that prior work has driven near 100% ASR on structured tasks. Semantic validators burn extra inference and reject benign traffic; TLS does not help when a legitimate intermediary terminates it. MIRROR is a communication-layer integrity primitive: it ships one canonicalized payload across *k* logical routes and accepts the message only when a strict majority of routes agree on the same digest. The hash is unkeyed, so integrity lives entirely in the assumption that honest routes form a majority (α < 0.5), including under correlated failures where what matters is the largest shared-failure group. Across MMLU, HumanEval, and MBPP on two frameworks and four topologies, and in a MetaGPT deployment against a production API, MIRROR drops ASR to 0% below that threshold at 1× LLM token cost; an LLM-as-a-Judge baseline costs 35× in the same deployment and blocks up to 44.2% of benign outputs in the topology sweep.
