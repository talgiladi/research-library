## Coding-agent efficiency and verification

### [Claude Code's practical guide: shrink the context before shrinking the model](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/costs)

The Code's Sunday special turns the recent backlash against token-heavy coding into a practical workflow, but the independently readable part is Anthropic's own guide, not its tweet-based savings anecdotes. Rather than treating every task as a reason to use a weaker model, the documentation recommends keeping unrelated work out of the conversation, reserving deeper reasoning for harder problems and preprocessing verbose logs before they reach the model. Hooks can expose failures instead of entire test runs, while focused subagents keep exploration out of the main context—although their own requests still consume tokens. Anthropic also documents usage attribution for skills and tools, making waste diagnosable rather than just a smaller allowance to endure.

### [Willison asks Claude to compare SQLite extensions, then challenge its first result](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/8/sqlite-parse-number-c-vs-rust/)

Simon Willison's latest experiment is a compact example of asking a coding assistant to investigate rather than simply implement. He had Claude Opus 5.5 build C and Rust versions of a SQLite number-parsing extension; Rust initially won on decimals despite producing much larger binaries. Asking Claude to close that gap yielded a C fast path that the resulting report measures at roughly 16–19 nanoseconds per row, about twice as fast as the Rust versions. The useful artifact is the [reproducible report](https://github.com/simonw/research/tree/main/sqlite-parse-number-c-vs-rust#readme), including correctness tests and eight million fuzz comparisons. Its code, prose and measurements are AI-generated and not independently reproduced here; the timings describe one small Linux VM, not a general language ranking.
