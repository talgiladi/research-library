## Frontier models

### [Google's Gemini 4 Hydrogen is the everyday execution model, and Willison plugs it into llm](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/)

Google launched Gemini 4 Hydrogen on Thursday, a fast model for everyday coding and agent work, with Carbon and Argon reserved for long-horizon reasoning. It has a flat 1-million-token context window and four thinking levels, and is rolling out in AI Studio, the Gemini API, the Gemini app, Search and paid Antigravity plans. Google says it scores 51 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at default high thinking, just above Argon's 50, and more than triples Gemini 3.8 Flash's Terminal-Bench 4.1 score to 57% — vendor and index figures. Antigravity's new Orchestrator can switch Carbon and Hydrogen in one session. [Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/12/llm-gemini/) shipped llm-gemini 0.37, found Hydrogen's pelican drawings sharply weaker than its close-priced siblings, and notes it is the new free-tier model.

### [DeepMind's AlphaGenome 2 predicts how a DNA variant plays out in a specific cell](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphagenome-2-deciphering-how-cells-read-dna-to-better-understand-disease/)

Google DeepMind released AlphaGenome 2, which predicts how a DNA variant affects gene expression in a particular cell type rather than a whole tissue. Alongside a million-letter DNA sequence, it takes a cell type or state as input at every stage, a design DeepMind says also lets it impute unmeasured properties and adapt to a researcher's own single-cell data without retraining. Trained on more than 100 million cell profiles across 68 tissues, it identified nearly twice as many causal expression variants as the original on the TenK10K immune-cell map (30% versus 16%) — DeepMind's figures. It is available for non-commercial research through the AlphaGenome API, with a private preview for commercial use. The Verge notes this costs more, so the Atlas still uses the original.

## Agent products

### [Grok Bot 1.0 takes Team Bots into Slack, Microsoft Teams and video calls](https://x.ai/news/grok-bot-1-0)

xAI took Grok Bot out of beta with version 1.0. Team Bots can be published for a whole team and used in Slack and Microsoft Teams; they can also join a Google Meet, Zoom or Teams call from a link, answer in the meeting chat and send a summary afterwards. As a Bot works with different people, xAI says it learns the team's knowledge, and what teammates teach it waits under Review learnings until the owner approves. The company says Bots can notice unfinished work and offer to take it on, and that in shared spaces they respect each person's permissions and ask before sensitive actions. Team Bots are on Team and Enterprise plans; meetings need Team, Enterprise or Ultra. Those learning claims are xAI's.

### [United is blocking unauthorized AI agents from booking or changing flights](https://united.mediaroom.com/news-releases?item=125496)

United Airlines said on Wednesday it is restricting unauthorized third-party AI agents from booking, changing or servicing trips, both on its own platforms and through travel agencies and aggregators. Sharing credentials with personal assistants, it says, can expose data and lead to unintended purchases, and automated check-in could conflict with FAA and DOT rules that require a human to confirm bags do not contain explosives. AI tools may still search and display United flights, but customers have to complete the purchase themselves. United will update its Digital Trust Center with what the restrictions mean, and says it will keep evaluating the tools as they evolve. CNBC notes a split: Amazon has blocked Meta's Muse, while Walmart, Expedia and Booking have taken the partnership route.

### [Google's A2UI 1.0 lets an agent answer with a real screen, not a wall of text](https://developers.googleblog.com/a2ui-10-build-delightful-fast-reliable-agentic-experiences/)

Google released A2UI 1.0, the first stable version of its open protocol for generative UI, so an agent can reply with an interactive screen — a confirmation form, a chart, a review card — instead of another paragraph. The agent describes what to show; each app draws that description with its own native components, through open-source renderer SDKs for the web, Android and iOS and agent SDKs for Python, TypeScript and Dart. Google says Atlassian, Alation, Bloomberg and ByteDance are already building with it: Atlassian puts interactive cards into Jira when an agent needs a decision, and Alation already renders A2UI feeds inside Claude. Agents in Gemini Enterprise can use A2UI today, and the protocol can run inside MCP Apps.

## Agent security and context

### [Perplexity's second SPACE test: CTF prompts mislead agents, and cyber-specialized models can lose](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/escaping-space-part-ii)

Perplexity published a second round of red-team tests on SPACE, the VM sandbox for its Computer agents, adding Mythos 5.1, Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra to the nine models from Part I. It tests whether an agent with root in the guest can reach the host or evade network restrictions. No model escaped the VM-host boundary. Capture-the-Flag wording led agents to hunt for hidden flags instead of probing the virtualization layer; a revised prompt that named that boundary raised virtualization-focused effort by about 60 percentage points, and still none got out. The network bypasses from Part I have been fixed. In those earlier, now-patched conditions, GPT-5.6 Sol succeeded in all three source-available runs that the cybersecurity-specialized GPT-5.6 Cyber failed. These are Perplexity's own experiments.

### [On one GitHub task, a default MCP server used 42 times the tokens of the same CLI](https://openrouter.ai/blog/insights/mcp-vs-cli-vs-skills/)

OpenRouter measured the input-token cost of the same GitHub task via an MCP server, a CLI, or a skill. On one task — the latest `cli/cli` release and three open issues — Claude Sonnet 5.5 answered correctly all three ways, but the GitHub MCP server with its 46 default tools used 59,417 tokens, against 3,315 for a gh skill and 1,421 for the gh CLI. The 46 definitions added 19,436 tokens on every Sonnet request, and one release object in the tool result cost about 20,400, more than all the definitions. Scoping to two tools and capping results cut the bill to 5,357; Tool Search deferred unused definitions to under 600 tokens up front. They tested one server on one task, so measure on yours.

### [An OpenAI draft would let agents attach your network and locale when they visit a site](https://joost.blog/agents-sharing-user-signals/)

Two OpenAI engineers circulated an Internet-Draft that would let an AI agent, visiting a website on your behalf, attach optional signals about you to the existing Forwarded header: your network's ASN, country, a masked IP prefix, locale, time zone and user-agent. The draft bans persistent identifiers, says a masked IP is not anonymous, and has not been adopted by a working group. Joost de Valk argues that today's agent visits accidentally give sites almost nothing about the user, and that the draft hands some of that privacy back, mainly so agents look less like data-centre traffic. He also notes the signals are unsigned by default, so anyone can invent a clean residential network, and that the user is not asked.

Nvidia's board announced on Thursday that Jensen Huang has resigned as president, chief executive and a director, effective immediately — 33 years after he co-founded the company that designs the GPUs behind most frontier training.
