### 6. [How to customize a model with LoRA and QLoRA](https://blog.bytebytego.com/p/how-to-customize-a-model-to-learn)

Primary link: https://blog.bytebytego.com/p/how-to-customize-a-model-to-learn
ByteByteGo’s guide starts where prompts and RAG stop: when a model has the underlying skill but won’t consistently apply a house style, classification boundary, or summary shape, supervised fine-tuning writes the behavior into default weights instead of stuffing more examples into every request. Full fine-tuning is memory-hungry; LoRA freezes the base and trains small low-rank adapters (two compact matrices whose rank — often 8–64 — trades capacity for cost) so only a tiny parameter set updates while the original model still does the heavy compute. QLoRA goes further by storing the frozen base in 4-bit form and keeping adapters at higher precision, shrinking the memory wall enough to adapt larger models on a fixed GPU budget — adapters learn against the quantized combination, so they can recover some compression loss without magically undoing every quantization error. The practical workflow is familiar: baseline a good prompt, build clean train/val/test splits, watch for overfitting across a few epochs, then either serve adapters beside one shared base or merge them into a standalone checkpoint. Retrieval still owns facts that change; fine-tuning owns recurring behavior.

## Agent memory

### [EnSIMem: Entity-Structured Indexing for Long-Term Agent Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27279)

Meng, Fan, Fan, Guo, Xie, and Han argue long-horizon agents fail when memory collapses into generic summaries or anonymous chunks that blur who said what about which entity. EnSIMem builds theme-coherent episodes offline and indexes dialogue-grounded entries as [entity][entity type][property:value], each retaining source turns, timestamps, and multimodal fields. At query time the agent’s request becomes evidence requirements aligned to that index; entity-property lookup and adaptive retrieval gather what point, temporal, compositional, and aggregation reasoning need, and the answer is generated from preserved source evidence rather than a lossy digest. On long-term agent-memory benches the design keeps contexts compact while staying accurate and efficient online. Entity-index note for Tal: store property-typed facts with provenance, and retrieve by entity-property needs—not similarity over summaries.

### [Meet, Compare, or Abstain: LatWeave for Deterministic Multi-Hop Question Answering on Knowledge Lattices](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27225)

Ren, Fan, Wang, Yan, and Han want multi-hop QA that does not smuggle “what is known” and “how to reason” into one probabilistic blob—hallucination stays endemic, evidence chains stay unauditable, and systems answer when they should refuse. LatWeave compiles knowledge into a multidimensional lattice and reduces answering to three deterministic operators: meet (constraint intersection), compare (lattice-order comparison), and abstain (structural refusal). LLMs appear only for one-shot extraction and query planning; the answer path is zero-LLM, zero-task-training, and recomputable end to end. On complete knowledge (MetaQA) meet chains stay near-lossless over three hops (any-hit 0.9975); on 2WikiMultihopQA held-out they report EM 0.865 above structure-augmented RAG reproductions; on IIRC structural abstention hits 0.971 accuracy with a 0.029 leak rate, while MuSiQue and HotpotQA degrade outside the lattice envelope as expected. Deterministic-RAG note for Tal: reserve the LLM for lattice construction and planning, and make the answer path an auditable operator chain that can abstain.

## Agent harness

### [Compliant with Local Controls, Collectively Discriminatory. A Governance Architecture for Multi-Agent AI in Regulated Finance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27994)

de la Chica Rodriguez, Vera Diaz, and Delgado Romero warn that banks shipping agentic workflows in credit, fraud, collections, and compliance still govern component-by-component—each agent locally tested and authorized—while institutional risk lives in the joint behavior of many locally fine parts. They name the gap constitutional non-compositionality: local checks need not compose into bounded disparate impact, market integrity, or traceable accountability. ARIA is their finance-specific reference architecture across normative-accountability, execution-control, and assurance-learning planes, covering policy specification, population-level observed-versus-expected monitoring, bounded authority, runtime containment, adaptive policy change, and preserved human oversight. Two simulations show shared-signal thin-file exclusion under local controls and earlier warning from distributional monitoring under constructed drift, with a validation agenda rather than a production-effectiveness claim. Population-gov note for Tal: monitor agent fleets against expected collective distributions, not only each agent’s unit tests.

### [Compliant AI Infrastructure for Regulated Finance: A tiered multi-agent framework with DLT audit trails for financial operations in DACH](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27632)

Kurz and Magg treat regulation as an orientation layer for a multi-agent finance stack rather than a brittle rules dump. A matrix of regulatory intent and exposure feeds a governed policy compiler that emits prohibitions, obligations, and runtime budgets: prohibitions block externalisation, obligations attach artefacts with explicit admissibility criteria, and committee activation stays policy-driven so oversight scales with exposure. Evidence, decisions, and reason codes bind to a permissioned DAG with deterministic timestamps for replay and failure attribution; clause-level legal indexing with effective dates plus capability-based agent routing aim at DACH and wider EU portability. The pitch is assurance by construction—compliance embedded in execution and auditor-verifiable without abandoning proportionality. Compliance-harness note for Tal: compile prohibitions/obligations into the runtime budget, and put reason codes on a replayable ledger beside the agent transcript.

### [State-Grounded Conditioning: Wrapping User-Facing LLM Agents Where Direction Depends on Live State](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27606)

Liu, Yuan, Ruan, Zhang, Wang, and coauthors isolate a harness failure they call direction drift: the agent finishes the task, but the chosen direction no longer matches live user state—game state, session history, inventory. State-Grounded Conditioning (SGC) pushes that control out of the prompt into rule kernels over structured inputs via Perception, Grounding, and Interaction wrappers with explicit conditioning dependencies. On a 200-session in-game coaching benchmark (~1,000 assistant turns), Perception alone holds mean first-token latency at 1.5s versus 6.1s for a production tool-use PE-Agent; all three wrappers lift turn-level grounded accuracy from 61–70% to 96.7% and session-level from 20–26.5% to 83.5%, cutting session grounding failures by about 78% versus the strongest baseline. State-wrapper note for Tal: externalize live-state control into Perception/Grounding/Interaction kernels—don’t hope the prompt tracks inventory.

### [The Capability Manifold and ML Scaling Laws](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27588)

Zaidi and Hafeez argue classic scaling laws that map loss to compute, parameters, and data stop being enough once models ship inside agentic harnesses: equal loss can hide unequal reasoning, retrieval, planning, and adaptation. Their capability manifold is a multidimensional map from those downstream capabilities onto pre-training, post-training, and test-time resources through bounded scaling functions, with analytical Jacobians that quantify sensitivity and cross-resource interactions. As a first application they embed Kaplan- and Chinchilla-style laws plus test-time compute as trajectories on one shared manifold, so harness designers can talk about capability tradeoffs in the same language as pretraining budgets. Scaling-harness note for Tal: when you buy test-time compute for an agent loop, score the capability axis you care about—not the base model’s loss curve.

### [FDE-Bench: Evaluating LLM Agents for Deployment Environment Configuration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27571)

Ding, Zhan, Li, and Guo turn “make it run in prod” into a harness benchmark: 136 deployment-configuration tasks across Docker images, Compose stacks, and Kubernetes, in greenfield and diagnose-and-repair modes. Agents submit declarative artifacts that are rebuilt and redeployed in a pristine environment; four gated binary layers—build, readiness, behavior, conformance—use programmatic checks with no LLM judge, plus a release gate that rejects do-nothing, spec-transcription, and stub shortcuts. Seven models from four providers on the same four-tool scaffold resolve 52.9–75.0% of tasks; readiness is the dominant failure (110 of 313 unresolved episodes), repair beats greenfield by 30.7 points on average, and ten tasks resist every model. A practicing engineer directing Claude-Sonnet-5 hits 92% on a 25-task case study versus 72% autonomous. Deploy-bench note for Tal: score deployment agents on readiness and conformance gates in a pristine rebuild, not on whether the YAML looks plausible.

### [Just-in-Time Memory: Learning to Curate Task-Adaptive Memory for LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27334)

Zhou, Li, Liu, Yavuz, and Joty flip the usual agentic-memory habit of curating at write time—distilling a finished trajectory into a fixed reflection, skill, or workflow before the future query exists. That irreversible summary must serve many unknown downstream tasks and creates a long-horizon credit-assignment mess for any learned curator. Just-in-Time Memory (JitMem) keeps raw trajectories and defers curation to read time: given retrieved traces and the new task, a curator synthesizes a compact task-adaptive payload consumed on the same episode, so training signal is immediate success rather than delayed utility. Across ALFWorld, WebShop, and τ²-bench, JitMem beats no-memory and both heuristic and learned write-time methods by 16.2, 16.3, and 3.9 absolute success points over the strongest baseline; even an untrained curator already rivals those baselines, with training compounding the gain. Read-time note for Tal: store raw traces and curate the memory payload for the current task—don’t freeze a query-blind summary at write.

## LLM security

### [Psychoacoustically Aligned Latent Smoothing for Adversarial Robustness of Full-Duplex Speech-to-Speech Dialogue Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27378)

Shamsaie and Modarressi attack the open mic of full-duplex speech-to-speech agents: end-to-end models that listen and speak at once leave a continuously exposed acoustic channel. They formalize imperceptible attacks as additive perturbations under the psychoacoustic masking threshold, aimed at semantic hijacking, response suppression, and policy jailbreaking—white-box success reaches 91.7% on an undefended Moshi-style agent. Their defense, psychoacoustically aligned latent smoothing (PALS), injects anisotropic Gaussian noise shaped by local codebook covariance at the RVQ latent interface, with masking-threshold-shaped input noise and a KL consistency objective, at zero inference-time cost. Hijack falls to 8.3%, mute to 11.2%, and jailbreak to 9.1% with clean quality within 2.3%; a Monte Carlo-smoothed variant certifies an ellipsoidal latent radius up to 0.616. Speech-jailbreak note for Tal: if the agent is full-duplex audio, treat the open channel as an attack surface and harden at the latent interface—not only the text safety layer.

### [Dynamic Deep Prompt Optimization for Defending Against Jailbreak Attacks on LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26185)

Obidov, Yu, Guo, and Yang push past static jailbreak defenses—fixed prefixes and suffixes that cannot track evolving attacks—without paying for safety fine-tunes that forget capabilities. Dynamic Deep Prompt Optimization (DDPO) treats the target LLM’s own intermediate layers as feature extractors: a lightweight MLP dynamically generates defensive embeddings from the current input and injects them into a later intermediate layer, so the defense is input-dependent while weights stay frozen. Across models and attacks, DDPO beats static prompt optimization especially on weakly aligned models and on semantically ambiguous benign prompts that static defenses over-block. Deep-prompt note for Tal: prefer input-conditioned intermediate embeddings over a frozen safety prefix when jailbreaks and benign edge cases both matter.

### [Semi-Automated Detection of Gaps in LLM Security Knowledge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18496)

Chai, Sun, and Staddon want a cheaper way to find where an LLM’s security knowledge is thin than hand-building challenge corpora. Their partially automated method mines authoritative Consumer Protection Agency material and flags response instability as a signal of knowledge gaps, demonstrated on identity theft and impostor scams across Gemini and GPT family models using public guidance from six CPAs. The method separates models that can accurately recognize those topics in narratives from ones that cannot, cutting the expert labor usually spent inventing security quiz items. Knowledge-gap note for Tal: probe security-topic instability against CPA source text before you trust a model as a scam/ID-theft advisor.
