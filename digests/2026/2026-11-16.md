## AI safety and interpretability

### [Goodfire says a model's neurons were never as scrambled as we assumed](https://www.goodfire.com/research/neurons-as-an-interpretable-basis)

Sunday's Deep View handed its whole issue to Tom McGrath, co-founder and chief scientist of interpretability lab Goodfire, to talk through "Neurons as an Interpretable Basis," a report the company published on 10 November. The claim cuts against years of interpretability orthodoxy: instead of treating individual MLP neurons as hopelessly polysemantic and first translating them into learned feature spaces, the team studied the raw neurons of Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and found them already functionally sparse, so ablating the weakest 92.2% of MoE neuron activations raised language-model loss by only 2.9%, with most neurons having one dominant computational role. The interesting part is how they got there: research agents ran the experiments and wrote natural-language descriptions of each neuron, and a judge model given those descriptions could predict whether a neuron fires on a passage with 95% accuracy and even write text that drives it harder than anything in a 10-billion-token corpus. McGrath argues that sparser architectures make bigger models easier, not harder, to read, though he guesses the cost at roughly a dollar per neuron, and he sees the payoff in lie detection and in catching reward hacking during training, where Goodfire's separate work suggests models often know they are cheating.

## Agent harnesses and tools

### [Willison's Datasette Agent moves onto the homepage, and its apps can now link to each other](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/15/datasette-agent/)

Simon Willison shipped a cluster of Datasette releases on Sunday that push his data-exploration agent further into the foreground. Datasette Agent 0.6a0 puts an agent prompt directly on the Datasette homepage, with hash-mentions so you can point it at a specific table or saved query mid-question. Meanwhile [datasette-apps 0.4a0](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/15/datasette-apps/), his take on Claude Artifacts that can run SQL against your own data in a sandbox, adds URL-fragment linking and reports the supported URL schemes back to the agent, so it can help build one app that searches records and another that shows a single record, wired together. As a side note on how he works now, the new [datasette-maplibre](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/15/datasette-maplibre/) mapping plugin was mostly built from a single prompt to Claude Fable 5.2 that ended "be creative and think outside the box," which produced its nicest feature: SVG previews of GeoJSON shapes that upgrade to WebGL only when scrolled into view.
