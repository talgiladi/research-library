## Newsletters

### [Microsoft's Jacob Andreou on AI's product gap](https://therundownai.beehiiv.com/p/microsoft-s-jacob-andreou-on-ai-s-product-gap)
In a sponsored Rundown AI Q&A, Microsoft Copilot EVP Jacob Andreou explains how Copilot was rebuilt as an “OS for work” around Home, Code and Autopilot. Packing AI into every Windows app brought traffic but little value; fewer entry points raised usage per user. Consumer Copilot Tasks once felt magical, then “an inch deep” on hard jobs like financial planning. No single lab wins everything: OpenAI’s 5.6-class models shine in Excel, Anthropic’s still fit PowerPoint, so Auto mode routes by task. He calls this an “overhang,” where Astra and Fable lack the form factors that made GPT-3 and Opus 4.5 useful. One-way doors—contacting a person, deleting a file—need a human; Autopilot locks the browser session as its boundary and re-asks if it tries another approach.

### [Why every company now needs to think and operate like a lab team | Josh Woodward (Google Labs, Gemini, AI Studio)](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/why-every-company-now-needs-to-think)
Lenny Rachitsky’s latest podcast sits down with Josh Woodward, who after 17 years at Google now runs Google Labs, the Gemini app and AI Studio, with products including NotebookLM (now Gemini Notebook), Project Genie and Flow. Topics include why every company should operate like a labs team, where the best ideas come from, Woodward’s “almost possible” framework, spotting genuine product-market fit early, when to kill an idea, which skills are rising, why roles are not collapsing into one universal builder, and how to stand up an internal labs team. The emailed newsletter and the open web page both stop before Lenny’s written takeaways, which sit behind the paywall, so this digest covers only the framing and topic list.
